# Frontier Models Are Becoming Eval-Quality-Limited

The strongest teams will keep their best evals private. That does not mean the standards for building evals should stay private.

The public benchmark era rewarded teams for finding a score that moved. The frontier era rewards teams for knowing whether a score is stable, reproducible, and meaningful enough to gate a release. That moves the bottleneck from benchmark count to evaluation quality: rubric clarity, criterion weighting, reviewer agreement, judge calibration, leakage controls, and release-to-release comparability.

AuraOne's open-source position is simple: private evals need public standards. EvalKit gives away the tooling layer so teams can inspect the judgment infrastructure before they trust it.

This post is a thesis piece, not package documentation and not a benchmark announcement. AuraOne is not claiming an expert-authored public benchmark in this launch.
